# The Curriculum

**Ten courses. One year. Built for teen nieces and nephews.**

This is a self-paced curriculum covering the ten skills that matter most for African teens growing up between now and 2030. Each course is built around real practice — not theory you memorize and forget. Every module has exercises. Every course has a capstone project that produces something real.

Read them in order if you're starting from scratch. Jump around if you already know some of this. Come back to any of them whenever you need a refresher.

---

## The ten courses

### [Course 1: AI Literacy and Prompt Engineering](01-ai-literacy.md)

Ages 13–19 · ~10 hours · 8 modules

What AI actually is and isn't. How to write prompts that get real results. How to spot when AI is lying. How to use AI for school without losing your ability to think. How to build small AI tools without code. Digital safety in an AI-saturated world.

### [Course 2: Financial Literacy and Personal Economics](02-financial-literacy.md)

Ages 13–19 · ~12 hours · 8 modules

What money actually is. How to budget without it feeling like punishment. Why compound interest will quietly make you rich. Investing basics with African examples. How debt traps people. How to earn your first real money. Taxes, currency, inflation. Wealth thinking and your 5-year plan.

### [Course 3: Coding Fundamentals — Python and JavaScript](03-coding-fundamentals.md)

Ages 14–19 · ~20 hours · 8 modules

How code actually works. Python from zero to building real scripts. JavaScript from zero to building interactive web pages. How to use AI to learn coding faster without becoming dependent. Git, GitHub, and shipping real projects.

### [Course 4: Persuasive Writing and Clear Communication](04-persuasive-writing.md)

Ages 13–19 · ~10 hours · 8 modules

Why most writing fails. How to write clear sentences and tight paragraphs. Persuasion without manipulation. Writing for different formats — essays, social media, emails, applications, pitches. Editing — the real skill. Finding your voice.

### [Course 5: Public Speaking and Storytelling](05-public-speaking.md)

Ages 13–19 · ~8 hours · 8 modules

Handling fear that doesn't disappear. Voice, body, presence. Storytelling — the highest leverage skill in speaking. How to structure talks that land. Presentation craft. Pitching and persuading live. Capstone: deliver a real talk to real people.

### [Course 6: Digital Entrepreneurship Including Sales](06-digital-entrepreneurship.md)

Ages 15–19 · ~15 hours · 8 modules

How to spot real opportunities. How to validate before building. How to actually sell — the skill most teens skip. Pricing and packaging. Marketing for the broke. Capstone: launch and make real sales.

### [Course 7: Data Literacy and Basic Statistics](07-data-literacy.md)

Ages 14–19 · ~10 hours · 8 modules

How to read any chart like a pro. What averages, medians, and percentages really mean. Probability and why humans are bad at it. How to spot manipulated data. Studies and surveys — what they really show. Spreadsheets for analyzing your own life.

### [Course 8: A Strategic Second Language](08-second-language.md)

Ages 13–19 · ongoing practice · 8 modules

Why bilingual French-English is the highest-ROI skill for African teens. How languages are actually learned. Building vocabulary that sticks. Listening, speaking, reading, writing — what each requires. Using AI and tech to accelerate. Your year-one language plan.

### [Course 9: Mental Health, Sleep, and Attention Hygiene](09-mental-health.md)

Ages 13–19 · ~8 hours · 8 modules

The most important course in this series. How your teen brain actually works. Sleep — the most underrated performance enhancer. Attention in a world built to steal it. Anxiety, low mood, and when to ask for help. The body running the mind. Building habits that compound for decades.

### [Course 10: Critical Thinking, Media Literacy, and Systems Thinking](10-critical-thinking.md)

Ages 14–19 · ~12 hours · 8 modules

The course that ties everything together. Logic and reasoning basics. The cognitive biases that fool everyone. Media literacy in 2026. How to evaluate sources, including AI. Systems thinking — seeing how the world really works. How to disagree well and change your mind.

## How to use this curriculum

**If you're starting from zero:** Take them in order. Each course builds on habits and ideas from the previous ones. Spend a month per course at a sustainable pace.

**If you have specific goals:** Cherry-pick. Want to start a business? Courses 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 cover most of what you need. Want to immigrate to Canada? Courses 4, 5, 8 plus Course 9 for mental resilience. Want to become a developer? Courses 1, 3, 7 plus 4 for clear communication.

**If you've taken similar things before:** Skim the modules you know. Do the exercises. Most of the value lives in the exercises, not the reading.

**For all of you:** finish what you start. A half-finished course teaches you nothing. A fully-done course (including the capstone) changes how you operate.

## A note on tone

These courses are written peer-to-peer, not teacher-to-student. The voice is direct, sometimes blunt, occasionally funny. The examples come from Lagos, Abidjan, Dakar, Cameroon — not San Francisco or London. The references include WhatsApp Business and mobile money, not just Stripe and PayPal.

That's deliberate. Most online learning content treats African teens as an afterthought. You're not. You're the audience this was built for.

---

## What's not in here

These courses don't cover everything. Notable gaps you'll have to fill elsewhere:

- Specific school subjects (math, sciences, history) — your school does that
- Spiritual or religious formation — your family and community handle that better than any curriculum
- Trade skills (electrical work, plumbing, mechanics) — learn through apprenticeship if relevant
- Athletic and physical skill development — practice and coaching
- Driving — schools and parents

The ten courses cover what's most poorly served by traditional education and most needed for the next decade.
